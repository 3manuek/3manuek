The present study case born out of the need for evaluating a strategy for enhancing writes on a solely and small
(~500MB) table highly updated, mostly with Hot Updates.

By intuition, Fillfactor could enhance performance due to less IO needed, however [spoiler] I've found that it was not the case.

The benchmark consisted in 3 strategies:
- Fillfactor=50 in both table and index.
- Hash partitioning.
- Just default schema.


>
> Version 18.2


## Findings

- Hash Partitioning has almost no overhead compared with the default schema.
- Fillfactor=50 has a significant penalty. 
  - It allocates ~7x more buffers in the shared buffer cache, and almost the same ratio of dirty buffers.
  - At the beginnning of the benchmark, it is obsereved that it might have better performance if using fillfactor by default on indexes.
  - Dead tuples is lower, but the amount of dirty buffers do not compensate overall.
